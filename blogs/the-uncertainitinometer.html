The Uncertainitinometer

Hastings looked, in disbelief, at the person sitting before him. It did not make sense, for he had attended the funeral of this particular individual in question over a year ago.

"How are you still alive?" he asked.

"The curtain was drawn over your eyes, mon ami, not my life," replied Poirot in a disengaged manner, "A LifeBender3000 pill was all that was required."

Hastings looked around, puzzled; nothing made sense. He was in a futuristic room, so perfectly square, so flawlessly symmetric. The room was the only thing that calmed him, for it was exactly the room that Poirot would live in.

"Huh?" exclaimed Hastings.

"You see, mon ami, perhaps I owe you an explanation. I was tired of these people committing all these grave crimes. It seemed I would always employ my little gray cells too late. All my life, I've only thought if I could stop the crime before it occurred. During my various endeavors in catching the criminal, I came across this scientific prodigy, a guy named Tesla; he might as well be the Newton of our time. After a few interactions with him, I realized that it might be possible to create a device capable of alerting me of a crime that would take place. Come with me."

Hastings followed him into a room. In the middle of the room was a weird metallic object on Poirot's desk. It was about a square meter, with several metallic pieces clicking and moving. On top was a small screen; it just showed a number, a percentage. Currently, it is at 20%.

"Over all these years, I have come to know deeply about the psychology of a criminal. Just before committing a crime, there is always a deep uncertainty in the criminal's heart, he might not realize it, but nevertheless, it is there. With my dear friend Tesla's help, the Uncertainitinometer has been created. This device alerts beforehand of an uncertainty spike in a precise location; a value of over 90% implies a grave crime, the gravest crime, in fact, a murder."

As he spoke, the metallic object started clicking and moving faster. The value on top of the screen increased rapidly. It made a loud beep in a few seconds, and the number stabilized.

"99.42%!" exclaimed Poirot. "Only one thing can have a number that high, an impending suicide!".

He pulled a couple of levers, and an address popped up on the screen.

"Oh my, if it isn't Harrison's place. Come on, mon ami, we must hurry. I happen to have another pair of Crocs27. Put them on in Sport mode."

Hastings put on what looked like a regular pair of Crocs with a button. He pressed the button and, to his astonishment, was lifted up in the air.

"What in the world!"

"Uncertainty, mon ami; Tesla didn't limit its application to finding a murderer. Here, grab my hand; we must fly quickly to Harrison's place."

They flew at a breakneck pace. Hastings had trouble digesting all that was taking place. It was as if he had been grabbed and yeeted 100 years into the future.

It was a few minutes before nine when they drew up to Harrison's house. Poirot quickly led himself inside the garden door, unlocked the main gate (Hastings did not even bother asking how he opened the door), and hurried inside the house.

They heard two voices from the balcony and rushed towards the sound. Upon reaching, Poirot immediately hurtled himself at Harrison (Hastings learned about the name later) and flicked the drink from his hand, which he was just about to drink. The glass flew high and landed on a wasp's nest growing nearby. Some of the wasps that were returning alighted, quivered a little, and then lay still. Other wasps crawled out of the hole only to die.

"Cyanide!" exclaimed Poirot. Turning to Harrison, he said, "Mon ami, you were about to commit a grave mistake."

Hastings looked at the second individual present; later, he learned his name to be Langton. Langton was aghast, and his face was now turning red with anger.

"You were trying to set me up!" screamed Langton.

"Mon ami, you need to remain calm," intervened Poirot.

Just then, an inspector also arrived on the scene, taking control of the situation. It seemed everyone was letting themselves inside the house.

"Ah the inspector has arrived, our job here is done," whispered Poirot to Hastings. "Let us go back."

Back at Poirot's uncannily symmetric house, Hastings was hoping for answers. All this new technology made no sense to him. He shot a lot of questions at Poirot.

Poirot chuckled, "That I leave as an exercise for your little gray cells."

There was a loud beep from the neighbouring room. The one that housed the Uncertainitinometer.

"Come on, mon ami, off we go again."
